# Ground rules
1. **Read-only.** Do not modify, rebuild or commit anything until I approve the findings. Write audit outputs only to the `audit/` folder at the project root.
2. **Evidence standard.** Acceptable evidence is a primary or official source: Irish Statute Book, gov.ie, Oireachtas records, EUR-Lex/Official Journal, EPA, SEAI, Revenue, the EEA PaMs database and the OECD CAPMF documentation. Record the exact URL, the access date and the specific section or article relied on. Secondary sources (law-firm notes, news, IEA) may corroborate but must not stand alone. Do not rely on memory. If no primary source can be accessed, mark the item UNVERIFIED and state what was attempted.
3. **Dates.** Distinguish announcement, adoption or enactment, commencement or entry into force, and first application. Also distinguish the dataset's start-year basis (`start_year_basis`), in particular "first positive OECD score" (flag `capmf_first_positive_start_not_verified_enactment`) versus a verified legal date. A difference is a finding only if the dataset or its text claims a date it does not support.
4. **Source records.** EEA descriptions and other source text shown on profiles are original records and must be quoted, not corrected. If they contain an inaccuracy, report it as an annotation issue.
5. **Known conventions are not defects.** The following are documented design choices: reported savings are repeated across years and sectors and used for selection only; coverage and bindingness are ordinal, researcher-assigned scores; CAPMF stringency is a policy-design proxy, not observed implementation; frameworks, portfolios and residual bundles are non-additive. Flag only text or data that contradicts these conventions.
6. Keep quotations from external sources short and attributed.

# Step 2: Verification against authoritative sources
For every policy family, verify its name, scope, start date, end or replacement date, and key amendments or rate changes. Give particular attention to:
- **Carbon tax:** introduction on motor fuels (December 2009) and other fuels (May 2010); solid fuels from 2013; rate steps by fuel type and effective date; sectoral scope.
- **EU ETS:** phases, coverage of Irish installations, inclusion of aviation, the Market Stability Reserve.
- **Climate Action and Low Carbon Development Act 2015 and (Amendment) Act 2021:** enactment and commencement dates; the Climate Change Advisory Council; the first carbon-budget programme, including the provisional 2031–2035 budget; sectoral emissions ceilings; the section 21 restriction on new petroleum authorisations and its saving provisions.
- **Buildings:** Part L editions for dwellings and other buildings (including NZEB); BER; SEAI programmes such as Better Energy Homes, Warmer Homes and community and business schemes; ecodesign and energy-labelling regulations.
- **Electricity:** AER, REFIT 1–3 (with PSO-levy support periods) and RESS auctions.
- **Transport:** CO₂-based VRT and motor tax (2008); Regulation (EC) 443/2009 and Regulation (EU) 2019/631 (fleet-average manufacturer targets); car labelling Directive 1999/94/EC; Biofuels Obligation Scheme; EV grants; the MAC Directive 2006/40/EC.
- **Industry and obligations:** the Energy Efficiency Obligation Scheme; mandatory energy audits (S.I. 426/2014); LIEN; Accelerated Capital Allowance.
- **Agriculture, LULUCF and waste:** low-emission slurry spreading, liming, peatland measures, the Landfill Directive and landfill levy, and any other policy present in the data. Audit only the policies the data actually contains. Note any major omissions separately, as context, not as errors.

Assign each policy one verdict: VERIFIED, DATE MISMATCH, NAME MISMATCH, SECTOR MISMATCH, SCOPE MISMATCH, AMENDMENT MISSING or UNVERIFIED. Assign each finding a severity:
- **Critical:** a factual error that changes the timeline or a score.
- **Major:** a factual error in displayed text.
- **Minor:** imprecise or ambiguous wording.
- **Note:** context only.

# Step 3: Timeline logic checks
- `status_in_year` agrees with `start_year` and `end_year` (with `end_year` inclusive) for every series. Check for gaps, off-by-one errors and post-expiry status.
- `intensity_index` lies in [0, 1] and equals implementation × coverage × bindingness. Residual rows equal the strict mean of their members.
- Where `implementation_source` is OECD-derived, implementation equals the same-year CAPMF stringency ÷ 10 (the mean of the matched codes), with no carry-forward or back-casting. Flag unexplained year-to-year jumps or drops.
- List every series and year using assumed timing. Confirm that the lag, ramp and half-life parameters match `config/timing_parameters.csv` and the Methods page. Confirm the timeline legend distinguishes assumed years as described.
- Multi-sector policies have consistent dates and names across sectors.
- Recompute the number of active series for each year from 2000 to 2023 and compare it with the charts and the text, including any claim about when growth occurred.
- Check that policies starting before 2000 keep their original start year, and that planned measures and post-2023 values are not activated.
