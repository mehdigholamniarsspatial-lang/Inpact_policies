Main caveat

Selection draws on reported or projected savings for 2024–2030, creating a retrospective-selection bias. Measures with large recent or projected savings are more likely to be included than earlier measures that have ended or lack comparable estimates. This is a selected policy record, not a complete historical inventory; use caution in causal analysis.

How the dataset was built

Two very different sources were combined into one year-by-year record of policies.

  1. 1

    Combine two sources

    Ireland reported {{ stats.eea_records }} policy records to the EU: {{ stats.eea_single }} individual measures and {{ stats.eea_groups }} group records that summarise others. The OECD scores {{ stats.capmf_categories }} policy categories ({{ stats.capmf_indicators }} detailed indicators) for 1990–2023.

  2. 2

    Screen candidates

    Five eligibility tests screened {{ stats.candidates }} candidate policies, and {{ stats.eligible }} passed. The reason for every decision is recorded.

  3. 3

    Select the core

    The selection targets at least 90% of positive reported savings among eligible measures in each sector where this can be assessed. No percentage can be calculated for land use, land-use change and forestry (LULUCF), where eligible estimates sum to zero. Named instruments linked to OECD scores ("OECD anchors") are also included. Smaller eligible measures form sector-specific residual bundles.

  4. 4

    Score every year

    Each selected policy–sector series receives an annual 0–1 intensity index. It multiplies an implementation proxy (OECD stringency or assumed timing) by researcher-assigned ordinal scores for sector coverage and how compulsory the measure is ("bindingness"). These scores describe policy design and scope; they do not measure observed implementation or effectiveness.

Emissions context

Compare each sector's 2023 emissions (left) with the number of selected policy series (right). Series counts do not measure policy ambition, effectiveness or the share of emissions addressed.

Agriculture was Ireland's largest emitting sector in 2023, mainly through methane from livestock and nitrous oxide from fertiliser and manure. The dataset includes two individually selected agricultural policies and a residual bundle containing a crude-protein reduction measure.

Energy-related sectors, by contrast, are covered by many overlapping instruments.

Differences in series counts reflect the selection rules and source coverage as well as policy history. The CAPMF extract used here contains no direct sector-specific stringency scores for agriculture, waste or land use. The chart is not a complete inventory of policies in those sectors.

How to read the data

  • Intensity is not emissions saved. It is a relative 0–1 index combining a policy-design or timing proxy with sector coverage and bindingness. It does not estimate tonnes of emissions avoided.
  • Reported savings are for selection only. They repeat across years and sectors, so never add them up.
  • Rows are not additive. Frameworks and portfolios can overlap with individual measures. Residual members are not listed separately in the core, but their bundle scores are averages, not additional emissions savings. Check each policy's "aggregation role" before combining rows.
  • Some timing is assumed. Assumed timing uses ramp-up and, where applicable, post-expiry decay. The timeline hatches assumed years for non-residual series from their recorded start. Residual bundles may mix OECD-derived and assumed values without hatching.
  • Selection uses recent assessments. See the main caveat at the top of this page.
