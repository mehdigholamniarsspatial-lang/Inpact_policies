How the dataset was built

Two structurally different sources were aligned into one annual policy panel.

  1. 1

    Integrate two sources

    {{ stats.eea_records }} policy records reported by Ireland to the EU, and OECD stringency scores for {{ stats.capmf_categories }} policy categories ({{ stats.capmf_indicators }} detailed indicators), 1990–2023.

  2. 2

    Screen candidates

    {{ stats.candidates }} candidates were assessed against five eligibility criteria; {{ stats.eligible }} were eligible. Every decision is documented with its reason.

  3. 3

    Select the core

    Measures reaching 90% of each sector's reported savings, plus named OECD anchors, form the core. Smaller eligible measures go into one residual bundle per sector.

  4. 4

    Score every year

    For each policy, sector and year: status, implementation (OECD score or assumed ramp-up), scope and bindingness, combined into a policy intensity index.

Emissions context

Ireland's greenhouse-gas emissions by sector in 2023, the final year of the dataset, alongside the number of policy series in each sector.

Agriculture is Ireland's largest source of emissions, mainly methane from livestock and nitrous oxide from fertiliser and manure, yet only three agricultural policy series meet the selection criteria. Energy-related sectors, by contrast, are covered by many overlapping instruments.

This contrast reflects both policy history and the coverage of the sources: the OECD framework has no direct stringency indicators for agriculture, waste or land use. It should be kept in mind when comparing sectors.

Interpreting the data

  • No tonnes are estimated. Policy intensity is a relative 0–1 index of how strongly a policy bears on its sector, not emissions avoided.
  • Reported savings are for selection only. They repeat across years and sectors, so never add them up.
  • Frameworks, portfolios and residuals overlap with other rows. Use the aggregation role on each policy before combining anything.
  • Some timing is assumed. Where no OECD score matches, a hypothetical ramp-up is used; these years are hatched on the timeline.
  • Selection uses recent assessments. Policies were selected using 2024–2030 reported estimates, which can introduce retrospective selection bias.
