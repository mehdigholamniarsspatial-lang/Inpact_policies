How Ireland's climate policies built up, year by year

This site tracks, year by year, {{ stats.core_families }} core policies that aim to cut Ireland's greenhouse-gas emissions and were in force between 2000 and 2023. It combines the policies Ireland reports to the EU (EEA database) with the OECD's policy-stringency scores (CAPMF).

It also includes {{ stats.residual_bundles }} residual bundles: one summary row per sector for smaller measures not listed individually.

Main caveat

Policies were selected using recent (2024–2030) assessments, which may bias the results. Policies that still report large savings today are more likely to be included than ones that faded earlier. Keep this in mind, especially in causal analysis.

How the dataset was built

Two very different sources were combined into one year-by-year record of policies.

  1. 1

    Combine two sources

    Ireland reported {{ stats.eea_records }} policy records to the EU: {{ stats.eea_single }} individual measures and {{ stats.eea_groups }} group records that summarise others. The OECD scores {{ stats.capmf_categories }} policy categories ({{ stats.capmf_indicators }} detailed indicators) for 1990–2023.

  2. 2

    Screen candidates

    Five eligibility tests screened {{ stats.candidates }} candidate policies, and {{ stats.eligible }} passed. The reason for every decision is recorded.

  3. 3

    Select the core

    The core holds the largest measures, which together reach 90% of each sector's reported savings. It also holds named instruments that the OECD scores ("OECD anchors"). Smaller eligible measures form one residual bundle per sector.

  4. 4

    Score every year

    Each policy is scored for every sector and year. The score combines how far it is implemented, how much of the sector it covers and how compulsory it is ("bindingness"). The result is a 0–1 policy intensity index.

Emissions context

Compare each sector's 2023 emissions (left) with its number of policy series (right): the largest emitter has few policies.

Agriculture is Ireland's largest source of emissions. Most of it is methane from livestock and nitrous oxide from fertiliser and manure. Yet only three agricultural policy series meet the selection criteria.

Energy-related sectors, by contrast, are covered by many overlapping instruments.

The gap reflects both policy history and the sources used. The OECD framework has no direct stringency scores for agriculture, waste or land use. Keep this in mind when comparing sectors.

How to read the data

  • Intensity is not emissions saved. It is a relative 0–1 index of how strongly a policy bears on its sector. No tonnes are estimated.
  • Reported savings are for selection only. They repeat across years and sectors, so never add them up.
  • Some rows overlap. Frameworks, portfolios and residual bundles repeat other rows. Check each policy's "aggregation role" before combining rows.
  • Some timing is assumed. Where no OECD score matches, the dataset assumes a gradual ramp-up. These years are hatched on the timeline.
  • Selection uses recent assessments. See the main caveat at the top of this page.
