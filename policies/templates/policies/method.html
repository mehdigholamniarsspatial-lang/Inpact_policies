Methods and data quality

This page documents how {{ stats.candidates }} candidate policies were screened and selected, how each policy-year is scored, which assumptions are involved, and how the dataset was checked for internal consistency.

Selection flow

Scoring model

Each policy p in sector s and year t is scored as follows.

Ip,s,t = Mp,s,t × Cp,s × Bp

I, policy intensity
Relative index on [0, 1].
M, implementation
Same-year OECD CAPMF stringency ÷ 10 where a matching indicator exists. Otherwise an assumed profile: M = clip((t − t₀ − lag + 0.5) / ramp, 0, 1), where t₀ is the start year. After expiry with evidence of lasting effects, M decays as 2−(t − tend)/h from its last operational level, with half-life h.
C, coverage
Ordinal legal-scope score: 1 (whole sector), 0.5 (major subsector), 0.25 (defined segment), 0.1 (narrow pilot).
B, bindingness
Ordinal score: 1 (enforced rule or statute), 0.75 (price or obligation), 0.5 (incentive), 0.25 (information or voluntary).

Aggregation rules

  • Residual bundles take the strict mean of their fixed members' intensities; a missing member makes the bundle value missing.
  • Framework (L1) rows are contextual and never added to instruments or programmes.
  • Portfolios and their delivery schemes are alternative representations; use one or the other.
  • No national total index is computed, because rows overlap by design.

Sensitivity index

The equal-weight alternative is the unweighted mean (M + C + B) / 3. A gated version is set to zero before a policy starts. The multiplicative form of the main index follows composite-indicator practice (OECD/JRC, 2008), but its weights are a stated convention rather than an empirical estimate.

Every candidate and its decision

Five eligibility tests: (A) mitigation purpose, (B) applies in Ireland, (C) an identifiable instrument or framework, (D) an identified start date, (E) active within 2000–2023.

The 90% materiality rule by sector

Within each sector, eligible EEA measures are ranked by their first available reported saving: ex-post 2024, then ex-ante 2025, then ex-ante 2030. They are selected in order until the selection covers 90% of the sector's positive reported total. The shares refer to reported totals, which may overlap, not to realised abatement.

Assumed timing profiles

When no OECD score matches, implementation follows a hypothetical linear ramp with a mid-year convention. After an expiry with evidence of lasting effects, it halves every half-life. Parameters vary by mechanism class and are scenario assumptions, not estimates.
