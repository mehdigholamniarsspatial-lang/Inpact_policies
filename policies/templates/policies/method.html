Methods and data quality

How each policy is scored

Each policy p in sector s and year t is scored as follows.

Ip,s,t = Mp,s,t × Cp,s × Bp

I, policy intensity
Relative index on [0, 1].
M, implementation
The same-year OECD CAPMF stringency score ÷ 10, where a matching indicator exists. Otherwise, an assumed ramp-up: M = clip((t − t₀ − lag + 0.5) / ramp, 0, 1), where t₀ is the start year. Some policies keep an effect after they end. For these, M halves every h years (the half-life) from its last level: 2−(t − tend)/h.
C, coverage
How much of the sector the policy legally covers. It is an ordinal score (a rank on a fixed scale, not a measurement): 1 (whole sector), 0.5 (major subsector), 0.25 (defined segment), 0.1 (narrow pilot).
B, bindingness
How compulsory the policy is, as an ordinal score: 1 (mandatory rule or statute), 0.75 (price or obligation), 0.5 (incentive), 0.25 (information or voluntary). It is a researcher-assigned classification of legal compulsion, not a measure of enforcement, compliance or effectiveness.

Aggregation rules

  • A residual bundle (one summary row per sector for smaller measures) takes the mean of its members' intensities. If any member is missing, the bundle value is missing too.
  • Framework (L1) rows give context only. Never add them to instruments or programmes.
  • A portfolio and its delivery schemes describe the same policy. Use one or the other, not both.
  • There is no national total index, because rows overlap by design.

Sensitivity index

As a check, an equal-weight index takes the simple average (M + C + B) / 3. A "gated" version of it is set to zero before a policy starts. The main index multiplies its parts, following composite-indicator practice (OECD/JRC, 2008). Its weights are a stated convention, not an empirical estimate.

The 90% selection rule by sector

Within each sector, eligible EEA measures are ranked by their reported saving. The first available estimate is used: ex-post 2024 (reported estimate of effects after implementation), then ex-ante 2025, then ex-ante 2030 (projected in advance).

Measures are selected in rank order until they cover 90% of the sector's positive reported total. Each bar shows the share reached in one sector. Shares refer to reported totals, which may overlap, not to actual emission cuts.

Assumed timing profiles

When no OECD score matches, implementation rises in a straight line to full strength, counted from mid-year. If a policy ends but its effects last, implementation then halves every half-life. Speeds vary by type of mechanism. They are assumptions, not estimates.
