Air quality policy

The fuels that drive Ireland's greenhouse-gas emissions also pollute the air people breathe. This section sets out the policies Ireland has used to control air pollution, from the 1990 Dublin smoky coal ban to the Clean Air Strategy's 2040 goal. For each policy it covers what it does, how Ireland has applied it, what is known about its effects on health, and how it connects to the greenhouse-gas policies in this dataset.

Scope

This is a curated reference record, kept separate from the scored greenhouse-gas dataset. The policies below have no intensity scores and are not rows of the 2000–2023 panel. Years are legal commencement or adoption dates from the Irish Statute Book, EUR-Lex, gov.ie and the EPA.

Why air quality belongs in INPACT

INPACT asks which policies have reduced emissions and improved climate outcomes. For air quality, the same question links policy to public health.

  1. 1

    The same fuels

    Coal, peat, oil and wood burned in homes emit CO₂ and fine particles (PM2.5). Diesel and petrol engines emit CO₂ and nitrogen dioxide (NO₂).

  2. 2

    The same homes

    Building regulations, retrofit grants, heat pumps and the carbon tax change how homes are heated. They affect smoke as well as carbon.

  3. 3

    Co-benefits and trade-offs

    Most climate policies also clean the air, but not all: wood stoves and the past shift to diesel cars show that the two goals can pull apart.

  4. 4

    Health outcomes

    Poor air quality is linked to about 1,700 premature deaths a year in Ireland (EPA, 2025). Policy timing is the starting point for health studies.

Using the platform for air quality and health

How to combine this section with the rest of the INPACT platform, and what is planned next.

What you can do now

  • Find the policies. Use this page for air quality measures and the Policies table (sector: Buildings or Transport) for related climate policies.
  • Map the sources. In Spatial Mapping, the MapEIre layers show PM2.5, NOx, SO₂ and other pollutants by sector on a 1 km grid, including residential combustion (2015 and 2019).
  • Check the timing. The Trend and Structural Break tools test whether an emission trend changed around a policy year.

Planned extensions

  • Tag every policy in the dataset with the air pollutants it affects and its health co-benefits.
  • Add the EPA's annual air pollutant inventory (PM2.5, NOx, SO₂, NH₃ by sector, from 1990), so trends can be tested for air pollutants.
  • Add measured concentrations from the EPA monitoring network, to link policy timing with air quality and, through partner studies, with health data.
